## 1. What It Is

The **DKC Digital Respect & Cohesion Fellowship 2026** is an **eight-month** youth leadership, innovation, and digital peacebuilding programme. **8 teams** — one from each of Bangladesh's 8 administrative divisions — will be finalized through a **three-stage evaluation** and receive the fellowship.

It is a youth-focused initiative implemented under the **Partnerships for a More Tolerant, Inclusive Bangladesh (PTIB)** project of the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)**. Since its inception in 2016, DKC has empowered young people through dialogue, creativity, and digital innovation, promoting social cohesion, resilience, tolerance, and peaceful coexistence.

DKC brings together young leaders, innovators, and community-level stakeholders to:
- Identify challenges in digital spaces
- Develop youth-led creative solutions
- Promote positive and inclusive digital engagement
- Address online harms: hate speech, misogyny, cyberbullying, and other harmful online behaviour

## 3. Eligibility

1. **Bangladeshi national**, aged **18–35**
2. Applying as a **team of 3–5 people**, all from the **same division**
3. Committed to **8 months of active participation**
4. **No prior professional experience required**
5. **Women and people from marginalized communities** are strongly encouraged to apply

---

## 4. Team Structure

- Teams consist of **3 to 5 members**
- If a team has **5 members**:
  - **3** participate as **core Fellows** in the bootcamps
  - **2** serve as **reserve support members** during campaign implementation
- Your team should represent, and plan to implement its initiative in, **one of Bangladesh's eight divisions**
  - This guarantees nationwide coverage rather than clustering in Dhaka
  - How "from" that division is defined (permanent residence, current study, or current work) should be confirmed against the application form

> **Important:** DKC is a **team-based** fellowship, not individual. You need 3–5 people because the fellowship is built around shared project design, divided responsibilities, and collaborative implementation.

## 10. Selection Process

Selection happens in **three stages**:

1. **Application Review** — All applications reviewed against criteria (problem understanding, solution quality, team diversity, etc.)
2. **Shortlisting** — Further screening of promising teams
3. **Interview** — Shortlisted teams are interviewed before final selection

> Only **8 teams** are chosen nationally — **one per division** — so competition is genuinely high, especially within divisions that receive many strong applications.

## 13. FAQ — Key Answers

**Q: Can I apply individually?**
No. DKC is team-based (3–5 members). The fellowship is built around shared project design, divided responsibilities, and collaborative implementation.

**Q: What does "from the same division" mean?**
Your team should represent and plan to implement in one of Bangladesh's 8 divisions. Confirm whether that means permanent residence, current study, or current work from the application form.

**Q: What can the BDT 50,000 be used for?**
Project implementation — production costs, workshop/event logistics, materials, community engagement. NOT a personal stipend. No matching funds required.

**Q: What if a team member drops out?**
Contact your DKC programme officer immediately. A team can continue with **at least 3 members**. Below that threshold, participation is at risk.

**Q: What about the bootcamps?**
Two residential bootcamps, both with **travel and accommodation fully covered**:
- **Innovation Bootcamp** (Oct 2026, 3 days) — refine ideas, build skills, create implementation plan
- **Progress Bootcamp** (Dec 2026, 2 days) — share progress, troubleshoot, prepare for showcase
- If 5-member team: only 3 attend as core Fellows; 2 stay as support members

**Q: What do I get at the end?**
A **DKC / SURGE / UNDP joint certificate** and membership in the **DKC Alumni Network**. Alumni can go on to mentor future cohorts.

## 🏆 Winning Strategy Notes

> These are strategic observations based on how the programme is structured:

1. **Division matters** — Only 1 team per division wins. If you're from a less competitive division, your odds improve. Research how many applicants typically come from your division.

2. **Three-stage evaluation** — You need to pass all three: written application review → shortlist screening → interview. Your pitch deck and written responses are the first gate.

3. **8-slide pitch deck is your weapon** — Cover these clearly:
   - **The problem** (specific to your division/community)
   - **Your proposed solution** (creative, digital-first)
   - **Target community** (be specific — who exactly benefits)
   - **Digital strategy** (how you'll use digital platforms)
   - **Team capacity** (what each member brings)
   - **Sustainability** (what happens after the 8 months)

4. **Theme + cross-cutting lens** — Show you understand both your main theme AND the cross-cutting lens. Weave them together in your proposal.

5. **Diversity wins** — They strongly encourage women and marginalized communities. If your team is diverse, highlight it.

6. **Local rootedness** — They want initiatives rooted in YOUR division's community. Show deep local knowledge of the problem and the people affected.

7. **Feasibility with BDT 50K** — Your proposal must be achievable with the seed grant. Don't overpromise. Show you can create real impact within budget.

8. **Commitment signal** — 8 months is long. Show your team is genuinely committed and won't drop out. Mention backup plans if a member leaves.
